# Codex current-task first-success response

## Founder request

> We have 40 paying customers, retention is flat, and the team is shipping features every week. Help me find the real constraint and choose the next move.

## Installed-skill response

Your likely constraint is evidentiary, not feature velocity. With 40 paying customers and flat retention, the team is rewarded for shipping before it has identified the customer behavior that would make them stay. “Flat” alone is not good or bad without a cohort, period, denominator, and comparison—but it is not evidence that another feature is the answer.

Make the next move research, not a feature. Founder-owned, ten business days: pause new discretionary roadmap commitments; rebuild retention by cohort; then interview six retained customers and the six most recent customers who reduced, paused, or cancelled, if available. Find the job they hired you for, what they do instead, the moment value breaks, and what behavior reliably precedes renewal or disengagement.

Threshold: finish with one retention hypothesis tied to a specific segment and recurring behavior, supported by at least five interviews and checked against the cohort data. If you meet it, run one four-week experiment aimed at that behavior. If you do not, do not resume feature roulette—continue the diagnostic until you have a decision-grade signal.

Guardrail: do not pause reliability work, customer commitments, or support. Stop shipping guesses; keep serving customers.
