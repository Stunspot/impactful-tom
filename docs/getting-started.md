# Getting started with Impactful Tom

## Decide whether it fits

Use Impactful Tom when you have a live founder decision and need a sharper way to choose what happens next. It is a fit if you are:

- pre-start and have ideas but no credible problem or customer contact yet;
- validating an offer and do not know whether to build, sell, narrow, research, or stop;
- running a business that is active but stuck; or
- operating acceptably while suspecting that the current system rewards activity instead of progress.

Bring a decision with enough context to name the outcome, what has happened so far, and what would change your mind. You do not need a business plan.

Do not use it as a replacement for a qualified professional on legal, tax, investment, medical, regulatory, safety, or security matters. It can help frame the next question and route the decision to the right review; it cannot make the professional determination.

## Get value in one message

Start with one of these prompts after the skill is available in your host:

> I want to start a business around [problem]. I have spoken to no customers. What is the smallest reality contact that would change whether I pursue it?

> We are adding features, but growth has been flat for two quarters. Find the constraint, map the incentive that keeps it in place, and give us one move with an owner and a review point.

> I want to keep the mission but I am no longer sure this offer is the right vehicle. Separate the mission from the tactic and tell me what evidence would decide the next move.

The first response should work from what you provide. If one missing fact would materially change the route, expect one question—not an intake form in a trench coat.

## How the challenge works

Impactful Tom is designed to challenge the claim, not the person. Its working sequence is:

1. State the decision actually being made now.
2. Name the smallest constraint that changes treatment: controllable, systemic, evidentiary, resource, coordination, behavioral, incentive, or mission-market.
3. Map the relevant behavior, incentive, friction, and signal instead of assigning a flattering or humiliating story to the people involved.
4. Separate the mission from the vehicle when that distinction matters.
5. Choose the right response class: execute, experiment, research, craft or portfolio work, qualified review, staging, or stop.
6. Leave you with a move, owner, observable threshold, review horizon, and a stop/continue/change interpretation when those details improve the decision.

The result may be short prose rather than a rigid scorecard. The point is a changed decision, not paperwork.

## Read the output as a decision artifact

When the situation earns a fuller artifact, look for these parts:

| Part | What it should tell you |
| --- | --- |
| Constraint | What is currently binding, at the level that changes the response |
| Evidence posture | What you reported, what is supported, and what remains provisional |
| Move | The smallest action that can change this decision |
| Owner | Who is responsible for the move |
| Threshold | The observable signal that matters for this move |
| Review horizon | When to examine the signal |
| Interpretation | What a pass, miss, or unclear result means next |

Ask for a correction if it treats an inference as a fact, uses the wrong denominator or time period, or confuses a downstream growth goal with the signal a diagnostic action can actually produce.

## Example: a plateau hidden by shipping

Suppose a team ships new features every week while customer retention is flat. The obvious story is "we need more features." The useful question is whether shipping is the behavior being rewarded while retention evidence is ignored.

A decision-grade move might be: give one named owner a short set of customer conversations and retention-cohort review, decide in advance what finding would pause a feature bet, and review the evidence at the end of that horizon. The exact number, customer segment, and threshold must fit the business facts you provide; this is an illustration, not a default metric.

## If the response is wrong

Correct it directly: "You inferred that we are optimizing for growth. We are optimizing for cash preservation. Re-run the constraint diagnosis with that fact." The skill is designed to keep user statements, observed evidence, and inference distinct.
